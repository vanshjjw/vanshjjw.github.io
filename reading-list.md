# Reading List

| Title | Author | Goal |
|-------|--------|------|
| A Theory of Justice | John Rawls | Use the veil of ignorance to derive fair principles of justice and the difference principle (inequalities only justified if they benefit the least advantaged). |
| Capital in the 21st Century | Thomas Piketty | Present empirical evidence on wealth concentration over time and argue for progressive taxation. |
| The Structure of Scientific Revolutions | Thomas Kuhn | Explain how science advances through "normal science" within paradigms and occasional paradigm shifts that rewrite the field. |
| Where Good Ideas Come From | Steven Johnson | Show how innovation arises from idea collision in "liquid networks" and the "adjacent possible." |
| How Innovation Works | Matt Ridley | Argue that innovation is driven by the recombination of ideas across fields. |
| The Medici Effect | Frans Johansson | Show that breakthrough innovation happens at the intersection of disciplines. |
| The Nature of Technology | Brian Arthur | Argue that technologies are built from other technologies in a combinatorial way. |
| Reinventing Discovery | Michael Nielsen | Explore how science can be reinvented through collaboration and how field boundaries are socially constructed. |
| QED: The Strange Theory of Light and Matter | Richard Feynman | Explain quantum electrodynamics accessibly; source of the "same damn thing again and again" quote about how we think. |
| Existentialism is a Humanism | Jean-Paul Sartre | Argue that we are "condemned to be free" and must create our own values. |
| The Ethics of Ambiguity | Simone de Beauvoir | Navigate moral ambiguity and argue that freedom comes with responsibility to others. |
| Fear and Trembling | Søren Kierkegaard | Examine the anxiety of choice, individual responsibility, and the leap of faith. |
| Thus Spoke Zarathustra | Friedrich Nietzsche | Overcome nihilism and create values after "God is dead." |
| The Absurd | Thomas Nagel | Give a short, analytic take on why we see life as meaningless yet still care. |
| The Myth of Sisyphus | Albert Camus | Defend absurdism: life has no inherent meaning but we can rebel and find purpose in the struggle. |
| Ethics: Inventing Right and Wrong | J.L. Mackie | Argue for moral anti-realism and show how we can still do ethics without objective values. |
| The Sources of Normativity | Christine Korsgaard | Explain why moral claims bind us even without objective foundations. |
| Reasons and Persons | Derek Parfit | Examine identity, ethics, and what matters for future generations and the long run. |
| The Precipice | Toby Ord | Make the case for existential risk as a priority and humanity's collective responsibility. |
| The Blank Slate | Steven Pinker | Argue against the blank slate; genes shape us more than we typically admit. |
| Blueprint | Robert Plomin | Present behavioral genetics evidence that DNA predicts life outcomes more than environment. |
| The Sports Gene | David Epstein | Examine nature vs nurture in athletic performance; 10,000 hours isn't enough. |
| Peak | Anders Ericsson | Present the science of deliberate practice (source of the "10,000 hours" idea). |
| Range | David Epstein | Argue that generalists often outperform specialists and that late specialization can work better. |
| Grit | Angela Duckworth | Argue that passion and perseverance matter for achievement—and probe how much grit is itself innate. |
| Talent is Overrated | Geoff Colvin | Argue that deliberate practice can close the gap—and ask how much. |
| Outliers | Malcolm Gladwell | Show how success depends on talent, circumstance, timing, and luck. |
| The Bell Curve | Murray & Herrnstein | Present a controversial but foundational treatment of IQ distribution and its implications. |
| Intelligence: All That Matters | Stuart Ritchie | Give an accessible overview of intelligence research. |
