# Reading List

Recommendations based on two essay ideas.

---

## On Taxation & The Social Contract

*Related to: government guarantees, progressive taxation, and the benefit principle*

### Books
- **A Theory of Justice** — John Rawls
  - The "veil of ignorance" thought experiment and "difference principle" (inequalities justified only if they benefit the least advantaged)

- **Capital in the 21st Century** — Thomas Piketty
  - Empirical analysis of wealth concentration and arguments for progressive taxation

### Talks & Essays
- **Elizabeth Warren's Social Contract Speech** (2011)
  - "Nobody got rich on their own" — articulates the benefit principle clearly

### Concepts
- **Benefit Principle of Taxation** — those who benefit more from government services should pay more
- **Public Choice Theory** (James Buchanan) — constitutional constraints on government spending

## On Innovation & Standard Machinery

*Related to: what counts as innovation, interdisciplinary conversations, and field boundaries*

### Books
- **The Structure of Scientific Revolutions** — Thomas Kuhn
  - "Normal science" vs "paradigm shifts" — directly maps to your "standard machinery" concept

- **Where Good Ideas Come From** — Steven Johnson
  - "Liquid networks" and the "adjacent possible" — innovation through idea collision

- **How Innovation Works** — Matt Ridley
  - Ideas "having sex" — recombination across fields drives progress

- **The Medici Effect** — Frans Johansson
  - Innovation happens at the intersection of disciplines

- **The Nature of Technology** — Brian Arthur
  - Combinatorial innovation — technologies built from other technologies

- **Reinventing Discovery** — Michael Nielsen
  - Collaborative science and how field boundaries are socially constructed

### Source for the Feynman Quote
- **QED: The Strange Theory of Light and Matter** — Richard Feynman
  - "...not because nature is self-similar, but because we have only been able to think of the same damn thing again and again..."

## On Meaning & Freedom

*Related to: nihilism vs existentialism, defining morals, freedom as fundamental*

### Core Texts — Freedom is Fundamental
- **Existentialism is a Humanism** — Jean-Paul Sartre
  - "Condemned to be free" — we must create our own values

- **The Ethics of Ambiguity** — Simone de Beauvoir
  - Freedom with responsibility to others; navigating moral ambiguity

- **Fear and Trembling** — Søren Kierkegaard
  - The anxiety of choice, individual responsibility, leap of faith

- **Thus Spoke Zarathustra** — Friedrich Nietzsche
  - Overcoming nihilism, creating values after "God is dead"

### Essays
- **"The Absurd"** (1971) — Thomas Nagel
  - Short, analytic take — we see life as meaningless yet still care

- **"The Myth of Sisyphus"** — Albert Camus
  - Absurdism — rebellion without hope, "one must imagine Sisyphus happy"

### On Consistent Morals Without External Authority
- **Ethics: Inventing Right and Wrong** — J.L. Mackie
  - Moral anti-realism + how to still do ethics

- **The Sources of Normativity** — Christine Korsgaard
  - Why moral claims bind us even without objective foundations

### On Species-Level Purpose
- **Reasons and Persons** — Derek Parfit
  - What matters in the long run? Identity, future generations

- **The Precipice** — Toby Ord
  - Existential risk and humanity's collective responsibility

## On Nature vs Nurture

*Related to: talent vs practice, human variance, what's innate*

### Core Texts — Nature Matters
- **The Blank Slate** — Steven Pinker
  - Against tabula rasa — genes shape us more than we admit

- **Blueprint** — Robert Plomin
  - Behavioral genetics — DNA predicts outcomes more than environment

- **The Sports Gene** — David Epstein
  - Nature vs nurture in athletic performance; 10,000 hours isn't enough

### On Practice & Deliberate Effort
- **Peak** — Anders Ericsson
  - The science of deliberate practice (source of the "10,000 hours" idea)

- **Range** — David Epstein
  - Generalists often outperform specialists; late specialization works

- **Grit** — Angela Duckworth
  - Passion + perseverance matters; but how much is grit itself innate?

- **Talent is Overrated** — Geoff Colvin
  - Practice can close the gap — but how much?

### On Human Variance & Distribution
- **Outliers** — Malcolm Gladwell
  - Success = talent + circumstance + timing + luck

- **The Bell Curve** — Murray & Herrnstein
  - Controversial but foundational on IQ distribution debates

- **Intelligence: All That Matters** — Stuart Ritchie
  - Accessible overview of intelligence research
